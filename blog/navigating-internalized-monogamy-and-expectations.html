Navigating Internalized Monogamy and Expectations

As someone who has navigated the world of polyamory and ethical non-monogamy, I've come to realize that one of the biggest challenges is not the external expectations of others, but rather the internalized monogamy that we all carry with us. This can manifest in a desire for exclusivity, a fear of rejection or jealousy, or a sense that our relationships are "not normal" because they don't fit societal norms. In this post, I'll share some practical advice on how to navigate these internalized expectations and cultivate a more open-minded approach to love and connection.

Recognizing Internalized Monogamy

Internalized monogamy refers to the ways in which we've absorbed societal norms around romantic relationships, often unconsciously. This might manifest as feelings of guilt or shame when considering non-monogamous arrangements, or a deep-seated desire for exclusivity that feels hardwired into our psyche. To start navigating internalized monogamy, it's essential to become aware of these patterns within ourselves.

One way to do this is by paying attention to your thoughts and emotions when considering non-monogamous relationships. Do you feel a twinge of guilt or anxiety? Do you assume that others will judge you for being in an open relationship? These are signs that internalized monogamy may be at play.

Challenging Assumptions

Once we've become aware of our internalized monogamy, it's time to challenge those assumptions. This can be a difficult process, as it requires us to confront the societal norms that have been ingrained in us since childhood. But with patience and self-reflection, we can begin to question these expectations.

For example, let's say you've always assumed that romantic relationships should be exclusive. You might ask yourself: "Where did I get this idea? Is it based on my own experiences or societal pressure?" By examining the source of these assumptions, you can start to challenge them and develop a more open-minded approach.

Practicing Open-Mindedness

Challenging our internalized monogamy is not a one-time task; it's an ongoing process that requires practice. One way to cultivate open-mindedness is by exposing ourselves to diverse perspectives and experiences.

This might involve reading books or articles about non-monogamous relationships, attending workshops or conferences on polyamory, or engaging in online forums where people share their experiences with alternative forms of love and connection. By expanding our understanding of what's possible, we can begin to see the world – and our own desires – in a new light.

Navigating Expectations with Partners

Finally, navigating internalized monogamy requires communication and honesty with our partners. When we're considering non-monogamous arrangements, it's essential to discuss expectations and boundaries openly and respectfully.

This might involve having conversations about jealousy, commitment, or the role of exclusivity in your relationship. By being transparent and honest, you can build trust and create a safe space for exploration and growth.

Conclusion

Navigating internalized monogamy is not a straightforward process, but it's an essential one if we want to cultivate healthy, open-minded relationships that honor our true desires. By recognizing our assumptions, challenging societal norms, practicing open-mindedness, and communicating with our partners, we can begin to break free from the constraints of internalized monogamy and create a more authentic, loving reality for ourselves.
